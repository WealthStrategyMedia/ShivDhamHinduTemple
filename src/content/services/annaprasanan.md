Annaprasanan celebrates an infant's first taste of solid food, traditionally performed around six months of age. The priest blesses a small portion of rice, often payasam, before the baby's first bite, and the ceremony includes a puja invoking health, growth, and prosperity for the child.

A well-loved tradition sometimes added to the day is a small selection of symbolic objects, such as a book, a coin, and other items, placed in front of the child. Whichever object the baby reaches for first is taken as a playful sign of their future inclination, whether toward learning, wealth, or another path.

The temple's priests perform Annaprasanan at the shrine or in your home, guiding the family through each part of the ritual.
