Lord **Shiva** holds the role of transformer within the Hindu trinity alongside Brahma the creator and Vishnu the preserver. Rather than destruction in a negative sense, **Shiva's** role represents the necessary turning of creation, preservation, and dissolution that keeps life in motion, clearing away ego and illusion to make room for spiritual renewal.

**Shiva** is most often worshipped in the form of the lingam, a symbol of his infinite, formless nature representing the union of cosmic energies. In his personal form he is shown seated in deep meditation in the Himalayas, matted hair crowned with a crescent moon, the Ganga flowing from his locks, and his throat stained blue (called Neelkanth) from having consumed poison to protect the world. His vehicle is Nandi, the sacred bull, a symbol of purity and devotion that devotees traditionally honor before approaching **Shiva** himself.

**Shiva** was the first deity installed at Shri Shiv Dham, with the temple's founding lingam placed in 2002 and its grand opening celebrated the following year during Mahashivratri, a connection the temple continues to honor with its name. He is worshipped regularly during Pradosham and celebrated each year at Maha Shivratri.
